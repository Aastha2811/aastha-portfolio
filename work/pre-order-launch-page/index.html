Pre-orders for a product with no reviews yet

The brief

Launch "India's 1st moisturizer that works like a daily facial" with an early-access page that builds anticipation and captures intent before sale. The catch: a brand-new product with no reviews, where a standard landing page wouldn't stand out.

The concept

One explorable scene of the Foxtale pink salon, extending the campaign's visual world, with eight hotspots that each reveal part of the product story. I shared it as a working, clickable mockup so leadership could react to the real interaction, not a static design.

How it evolved

  • Visual direction: leadership wanted a top-down "Barbie salon" with the brand mascot for cheekiness; the brand team asked for chrome over brass and gold to match the PR kit. Aligned in a day.
  • Sign-up to pre-order: a pre-order page should be conversion first, so the CTA became a simple "Pre-Order Now" with the freebie as a small callout on a crowded mobile layout.
  • Copy and claims: wrote copy requirements across 9 surfaces, flagged a desktop/mobile CTA mismatch and claims needing legal sign-off, and kept glow and hydration claims separate.
  • Live, then iterated: hotspots weren't prominent enough, so I changed their effect and size, tuned tap targets for 390px, and led the ingredients with Vitamin C, Papaya Enzyme and 5-Ceramide Complex.
  • Said no to a clickable thumbnail on the bottom sheet: an image popup over a sheet feels broken on mobile.

Results, launch week

People want product info first, then "is this for me", then urgency. And there's a cliff after the first hotspot: most people open one and leave.

For the next launch

  • Design for the middle: put a second hook inside the first hotspot.
  • The product is always the hero hotspot; make it the most prominent entry point.
  • Urgency and personalisation close the sale; they don't open it.
  • Track interaction depth, not just time on page, and don't start A/B tests during launch traffic.
  • Pre-order works for a product with no reviews. It's worth turning into a playbook.

Caveats

  • Explorers chose to engage, so the 33% vs 21% gap shows correlation, not proven cause.
  • All results come from launch week, when intent is unusually high.
