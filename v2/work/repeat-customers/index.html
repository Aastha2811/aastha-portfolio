Turning first-time buyers into repeat customers

Most first-time buyers of our Glow Vitamin C serum (GVC) never ordered again. I went out to find out why, then built a guided journey around the moment people decide whether a product works.

The problem

  • About 2 in 3 customers don't place a second order within a year. Only ~31% ever repeat, so the first-to-second order step is the single biggest leak.
  • Retention is eroding as acquisition scales: ~35% for 2022–23 cohorts, ~27–28% for 2025 cohorts.
  • GVC is our #2 acquisition serum but the weakest retainer of any serum, with 13.5% 90-day repeat and the lowest LTV.
  • 54.8% of GVC buyers are new to Foxtale, and many are new to Vitamin C. The bottle runs out around Day 55–60, and the second-order window closes by ~Day 90.

Finding the why

The data showed people weren't coming back, but not why. So I asked one question across user calls, conversations near stores and malls in Mumbai, and a read of post-purchase CRM tickets: what happens between the first order and the reorder decision?

Key insight

Every user reaches a results judgement at week 2–3. That moment decides the reorder.

Good change

Trust forms and a reorder becomes likely.

Bad change

The product is dropped for good.

No change

The biggest silent loss. No signal ever reaches the brand.

Seven problems, mapped

StageProblemSignal
Pre-usageDelivery uncertainty: WISMO ~36%, cancellations ~21%Medium
Day 0–3Users work out usage themselvesStrong
Day 0–7Users focus on avoiding reactions, not on getting resultsStrong
Week 2–3No visible change, so users drop silentlyStrong
Week 3–6Every reorder is a fresh decisionStrong
Day 0–60No brand presence at key momentsMedium
Whole journeyBeginners have no success criteria or timelineEarly

I committed two interventions to leadership on 29 April: guided onboarding for Day 0–7, measured by week-1 active users, and a results checkpoint for week 2–3, measured by week-2 to week-4 continuation.

What we built

The existing WhatsApp flow was a message tree with no reason to come back. I stress-tested it against the Hooked model and rebuilt it as Skin Streak and Progress Mirror, then anchored it on GVC and the user's own skin.

  • Day 21 is the before/after scan: glow and hydration are visible by then, it's the habit sweet spot, and pigmentation is set as a 6+ week expectation instead of over-promised.
  • Day 42 is the refill nudge, timed before the bottle runs out.
  • WhatsApp was capped at two anchor moments to control cost.

Funnel fixes that moved numbers

Magic-link auto-login, live 29 Sep

The post-delivery WhatsApp link dropped users onto a login wall. Once people tapped Start, completion was ~60% either way, so the whole gain came from removing the login.

Onboarding A/B test, VWO, 583 users

A "21-day challenge" variant lost to Control, 20% started vs 28%, a statistically significant gap. Its "Remind me later" option gave people an exit, and only 2 of 26 came back. Control shipped.

  • V1 held up over a longer window too: 30% of completers bought again within 60 days, vs 19% of all GVC buyers.
  • The lift holds on app and web, for new and repeat customers, and is strongest among first-time buyers.
  • Most of it comes from other products. GVC rebuys move less, which fits a 30-day window that ends before the bottle runs out.

What's next

  • Scale to moisturisers, starting with the Brightening Moisturizer, then extend to NS12 buyers.
  • Move every post-delivery message to the magic link, roughly +35 scans a week.
  • Set up a holdout group for a causal read.

Caveats

  • Completers opted in, so they may have been more engaged to begin with. This shows correlation, not proven cause.
  • A holdout group is needed to prove the journey caused the lift.
